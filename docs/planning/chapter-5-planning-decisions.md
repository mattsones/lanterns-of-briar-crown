# Chapter 5 Planning Decisions

Date opened: 2026-09-04

Status: owner planning in progress; decisions are recorded one at a time before the executable vertical-slice contract is written

## Settled Decisions

### Climax And Ending

- Rescuing Lio during the Briarhold confrontation is the chapter's mechanical and emotional climax.
- Bracken's escape, the Lio–Mara reunion, and the surviving evidence resolve the Briarhold sequence without treating the rescue as a failure.
- The chapter continues to Bramblecross for a denouement with Mayor Anwen, Enna, Hollis, and Quill Pebbleturn. Enna's case-wall reading provides investigative closure and the next-arc lead.

### Return To Bramblecross

- The player will not manually retrace the Underway.
- No established public road directly joins Briarhold to Bramblecross, and Quill cannot predict which concealed surface exit the party will use.
- The party instead makes a clearly long overland journey east through the Rainroot woods and wilds.
- The return uses one short playable wilderness map with dialogue points that convey distance and the changing journey.
- The map may include a bounded wild-animal encounter and a practical Rainroot woods resident who trades news and directions with the party. Neither beat should rival the Briarhold climax.
- Quill waits in Bramblecross with the town leadership rather than intercepting the party on the road.

### Rainroot Woods Witness

- The return-map NPC is a Rainroot woodsman or trapper who knows the eastern terrain and can direct the party toward Bramblecross. He is a practical local witness rather than a faction expert or planned rescuer.
- He previously saw Princess Elowen during a public stop on her Royal Progress, either from a town crowd or beside a road where her party passed.
- He did not speak with Elowen and has no private knowledge of her plans. What he saw gave him a grounded impression of how she listened, behaved, or treated ordinary travelers.
- That memory makes the later contradictory orders feel suspicious without allowing him to prove who issued them or explain the Briar Crown's structure.

### Overland Return Sequence

- The short return map opens with the rescued group leaving Briarhold as night gives way to morning.
- An early dialogue waypoint compresses a much longer stretch of travel so the physical map remains short without making Bramblecross seem geographically close.
- The party then meets the Rainroot woodsman, trades news and directions, hears his public-witness impression of Princess Elowen, and receives a clear warning about the tusker hollow ahead.
- The player chooses between the shorter route through the territorial boars' hollow and a longer safe detour.
- Both routes reconverge before a final Lio and companion conversation and the approach to Bramblecross. Important political information and character development cannot be lost by choosing either route.

### Lio After The Rescue

- Lio remains a recovering story companion through the end of Chapter 5 and does not join the combat roster.
- During any tusker battle, he helps the other rescued prisoners reach cover and keeps Mara safe rather than fighting.
- Outside combat, he contributes route knowledge, firsthand information about the transferred route examiner, and his own perspective on captivity and Bracken.
- Chapter 6 is the earliest point at which Lio might become mechanically combat-capable. That remains a future decision rather than a promise that he must join the roster immediately.

### Return-Map Wildlife

- The wilderness encounter is territorial rather than Briar-aligned.
- The player can recognize the danger and take a longer safe route around it.
- Pressing through the animals' territory leads to combat, but the battle is never required to complete the return or the chapter.
- The encounter remains lower-stakes than Briarhold and should communicate local ecology rather than introduce another villainous threat.
- The animals are a pair of **Rainroot tuskers**, large regional boars feeding in a storm-fallen root hollow.
- They may be understood as the same broad kind of animal as the Bramble Boar near Hearthhollow. The recurrence gives the region ecological continuity rather than implying every chapter needs a wholly new monster.
- Churned earth, damaged bark, tracks, and warning calls make their territory legible before the player commits to entering it.
- The encounter remains distinct from Chapter 1 because the boars are found in their natural territory, threaten no settlement, carry no evidence, and can be avoided without abandoning anyone in danger.
- The animals are not corrupted, controlled, or secretly associated with the Briar Crown.

### Briarhold Map Topology

- Briarhold uses a compact hub-and-wings structure rather than one enormous map, a maze, or a wholly linear corridor.
- A small Outer Approach map establishes and opens the concealed waystation entrance.
- A central Briarhold Interior map connects three short wings: the False Ledger Room, Thornseal Workshop, and Captive Lantern Hall.
- The player can choose the order in which to investigate those wings. Discoveries and interventions can improve the eventual rescue rather than merely checking identical prerequisites.
- A separate Holding Cells and Crown Table area contains Lio, Bracken, and the boss-battle climax.
- Moving between rooms requires stealth or restraint. Raising attention changes later resistance but cannot create a hard failure that makes Lio permanently unreachable.

### Briarhold Alert State

- Briarhold uses a lightweight three-state alert system: **Quiet**, **Suspicious**, and **Alerted**.
- The current state is visibly labeled for the player and reinforced by footsteps, guard dialogue, patrol changes, and other environmental responses.
- Noise, visible fights, and careless interactions can raise attention. Disabling signals, finding service routes, and using companion strengths can preserve a quieter approach.
- Alert never creates an instant failure or makes Lio permanently unreachable. It changes resistance and how prepared Bracken's side is for the climax.

### Interlocked Wing Infiltration

- The Crown Table and holding-cell entrance is both locked and guarded.
- The player can attack the entrance guards and take their key. This is a valid direct route, but it immediately makes Briarhold **Alerted** and causes the remaining cell to prepare for the party.
- The quieter route requires discoveries from all three interior wings rather than treating the wings as unrelated optional bonuses.
- The False Ledger Room contains a locked desk drawer. The player does not initially know what it contains; opening it reveals a spare key for the guarded inner entrance.
- The Thornseal Workshop contains a small tool, spring, shim, or similar piece that is not the drawer's actual key but gives Mara what she needs to pick or bypass the ordinary desk lock.
- The Captive Lantern Hall provides a way to draw the inner guards away from their post. The exact signal, distraction, and relationship to the lantern-order puzzle remain to be decided.
- Individual guards and workers can be avoided, quietly bypassed, or overcome. Being discovered or choosing open combat raises the visible alert state instead of causing an instant infiltration failure.
- The quiet solution is an intelligible causal chain: obtain the improvised lock tool, open the ledger drawer, take the spare key, create the lantern-based distraction, and enter the inner room without sounding a general alarm.

### Captive Lantern Signal Center

- The Captive Lantern Hall is an old mechanical waystation signal center, not a magical speaking network.
- The interaction does not reuse the Witness Stones' established shelter, water, warning, and witness set.
- Several fixed lantern positions accept differently colored glass panes. After arranging the panes, the player pulls the signal shutters open to transmit the resulting code through physical light channels and linked shutters.
- The desired code is the standard **Front Gate Alert**, which draws the three or four guards away from the locked inner entrance to investigate the outer receiving approach.
- Other valid codes have different operational meanings, including at least one dangerous command-center recall that would send guards toward Bracken. Many pane arrangements are not meaningful signals at all.
- An invalid arrangement causes one guard to enter the signal room and investigate who is operating the equipment.
- Color remains the dominant presentation, but each pane also needs a distinct etched mark, edge shape, or frame notch so the puzzle is readable without relying on color perception alone.
- Restored gold light also reaches the Crown Table and reveals true route lines beneath Bracken's false seals. This visibly weakens or counters his false-command mechanic during the boss battle.
- The loud key-taking route and the quiet tool–drawer–signal route remain valid alternatives. The latter rewards investigating all three wings.

### Fixed Signal Reference

- The False Ledger Room desk has the standard signal chart secured beneath its glass writing surface. It is the same desk whose locked drawer contains the spare inner key.
- The chart is fixed in place rather than a collectible item. The player can inspect it repeatedly, memorize or externally note the useful codes, leave to operate the Lantern Hall, and return to review it if needed.
- The chart includes several real signals among many possible pane arrangements, making the distinction between the Front Gate Alert and the command-center recall meaningful.
- The fixed reference gives the Ledger Room immediate value even before the player finds the workshop tool that lets Mara open its drawer.

### Invalid-Signal Response

- The first meaningless signal causes one guard to leave the inner-door detail and approach the Lantern Hall alone.
- The player hears the guard coming and receives three clearly differentiated responses:
  1. **Hide:** attempt to remain unseen. Success leaves all guards in play and raises Briarhold only to **Suspicious**; failure exposes the party and makes Briarhold **Alerted**.
  2. **Prepare a direct assault:** confront the arriving guard without attempting concealment. This is certain and removes that guard from the later door detail, but the open fight makes Briarhold **Alerted**.
  3. **Hide and attempt a silent ambush:** make a harder stealth or precision attempt to incapacitate the guard quietly. Success removes one guard while leaving Briarhold **Suspicious**; failure makes Briarhold **Alerted**.
- The silent ambush is nonlethal in presentation and is never performed by Mara.
- The first guard does not carry the spare inner key, so deliberately drawing one guard cannot replace the Ledger Room and Workshop solution.
- A second wrong signal always makes Briarhold **Alerted**. After one unexplained activation, the guards adapt and no longer send a lone checker. This ends the quiet route but does not fail the puzzle, rescue, or chapter.
- The interface must state the stakes of each response before commitment and clearly warn, once Briarhold is **Suspicious**, that another wrong signal will trigger a general alert.
- **Hide** and **Hide and attempt a silent ambush** use visible dice-driven skill checks with their difficulty and consequences shown before the player commits. They use existing character stats rather than introducing a separate Stealth stat.

### Bracken Boss And Escape

- Bracken Voss is directly fought as Chapter 5's boss. A lieutenant does not replace him as the mechanically defeatable antagonist.
- The party genuinely defeats Bracken's control of the confrontation and pushes him to the point where he can no longer hold the Crown Table room.
- At the escape transition, Bracken creates an immediate physical threat to Lio. The party faces a clear tension between pursuing or finishing Bracken and saving Lio.
- The heroes choose Lio. Bracken escapes during the time required to secure the rescue, making his survival a consequence of the party's values rather than an ignored combat result or a failed objective.
- The scene must still feel victorious: Lio is saved, Bracken's cell is broken, and Bracken leaves wounded or stripped of his local command apparatus.

### Prisoner-Transfer Carriage Rescue

- Lio is restrained in a prisoner-transfer carriage on an old sloping route rail during the Crown Table confrontation.
- When Bracken loses control of the fight, he releases the carriage brake and opens the westbound transfer shutter.
- Lio actively resists by jamming or slowing the carriage long enough for the party to reach him.
- The party stops the carriage and frees Lio instead of pursuing Bracken. Bracken escapes wounded through a narrow personnel passage and seals it while the party is occupied.
- This is an authored moral decision rather than a menu option that allows the player to abandon Lio. The playable action is to stop the carriage and complete the rescue.
- Restoring the Captive Lantern signal channels carries gold light into the room and reveals the true brake markings beneath Bracken's false control plate, making the rescue cleaner and visually connecting the puzzle to the climax.
- The imagery deliberately answers the cell's practice of recording and moving people like cargo: the heroes stop the machinery because Lio is a person, not a transfer entry.

### Alert-Dependent Boss Support

- Bracken is never fought alone. The Crown Table confrontation includes one, two, or three supporting enemies depending on the alert state and the party's earlier choices.
- A Thornroot Sentry is Bracken's constant support and physically connects the fight to the route-control machinery and false-command mechanic.
- A quiet infiltration produces the smallest support roster. Suspicion can leave one or more Thornseal Guards with Bracken, while an alerted facility produces the largest and most prepared encounter.
- Successfully removing the lone investigating guard through a silent ambush must reduce the later guard presence in a visible way.
- Openly clearing the original door detail does not erase the alert consequence; alarms can bring remaining personnel from elsewhere in Briarhold to support Bracken.
- Exact support counts, duplicate-guard use, HP, and starting effects will be settled during the boss combat graybox. The contract-level rule is that quieter play produces fewer or less-prepared supports and Bracken always retains at least one ally.

### First Sight Of Lio

- Every route passes a mandatory inspection grille just inside the guarded inner entrance and before the Crown Table confrontation.
- Through the grille, the party sees Lio restrained beside the prisoner-transfer carriage while a guard forces him to copy courier route marks.
- Lio writes plausible but deliberately incorrect shorthand and quietly works one hand against the carriage mechanism. This establishes his active resistance and prepares his later ability to slow the runaway carriage.
- Mara sees that Lio is hurt but still himself. She nearly calls to him, and the hero or active companion keeps her quiet before the guard can notice.
- Lio briefly notices Mara without openly reacting. Their silent recognition precedes the spoken reunion after the rescue.

### Mara During The Crown Table Battle

- Mara enters the Crown Table room with the party rather than waiting in a separate inspection booth.
- She stays behind a substantial table, desk, or route-control console near the entrance and outside the main line of combat.
- Mara remains visible and emotionally present but receives no combat turn and is never an enemy target.
- At the appropriate moment, she shouts one warning or sibling clue that helps Lio resist and slow the released transfer carriage.
- Once the carriage is stopped and the immediate danger is over, she can reach Lio without an artificial barrier delaying their reunion.

### Briarhold Prisoners

- The five transfer rows established in Chapter 4 refer to Lio and four other prisoners.
- Lio and three other captives are still physically present at Briarhold and are rescued before the party leaves.
- At least one of the three is one of the missing Bramblecross cellar porters, closing a Chapter 1 thread.
- The fifth recorded prisoner has already been transferred farther west. That absence proves the wider prisoner route remains active beyond Bracken's cell.
- Any captive still physically present is part of the required successful outcome. Optional exploration may free or help them earlier, but the player cannot knowingly complete the chapter while leaving them imprisoned.

### Outer Approach Entry Routes

- The Outer Approach offers three explicit ways into Briarhold. The alert consequences are shown before the player commits.
- **Use the old service culvert:** a longer, reliable infiltration route discovered through environmental evidence such as runoff, discarded workshop material, and footprints disappearing behind roots. Enter Briarhold **Quiet**. An Instinct check may reveal the route sooner but cannot permanently hide it from any character build.
- **Slip through the receiving gate:** a faster route attempted while a supply cart or worker passes. A visible skill check succeeds into **Quiet** and fails forward into **Suspicious**; it does not accidentally create a full alert.
- **Attack the receiving-gate guards:** guaranteed entry with no skill check, but the player receives a clear warning and knowingly begins Briarhold **Alerted**.
- Every entry reaches the central hub without bypassing the False Ledger Room, Thornseal Workshop, or Captive Lantern Hall.
- An alerted player can still gain meaningful benefits from the wings. The workshop can suppress reinforcements, the spare key can avoid another forced door fight, and the Lantern Hall can weaken Bracken's false-command effects even though alerted guards will not follow its distraction.

### Briarhold Personnel

- Briarhold contains a deliberate mixture of committed cell members, deceived workers, and coerced labor.
- Thornseal guards and senior cell officers knowingly serve Bracken and protect the operation.
- A sealwright or route clerk may knowingly assist the forgeries without behaving like a frontline soldier.
- Ordinary laborers were hired under official-looking road orders and understand only fragments of Briarhold's true purpose.
- At least one prisoner is being forced to work. These distinctions create opportunities to avoid, warn, persuade, or rescue people rather than treating every occupied room as a mandatory battle.

### Westroot Route-Record Clerk

- One of the three non-Lio captives still at Briarhold is a Westroot route-record clerk whom the cell abducted for knowledge of Westroot account marks and signal procedure.
- The clerk has been forced to work near the False Ledger Room and can explain how the cell reproduced local procedural details without turning the character into a willing traitor.
- Once freed, the clerk can identify that a surviving criminal instruction is written on genuine office stock and carries authentic internal correction or handling marks.
- The clerk cannot identify the compromised official or independently explain the faction's full hierarchy. Enna later combines this narrow authentication with the other evidence at the Bramblecross case wall.
- The four people rescued from Briarhold are Lio, a missing Bramblecross cellar porter, this Westroot clerk, and the Rainroot road warden. The fifth ledger prisoner has already been transferred west.

### Lio And Mara Reunion Cadence

- Mara reaches Lio as soon as the runaway transfer carriage is stopped, giving the rescue an immediate emotional release rather than postponing their reunion behind cleanup.
- Their first exchange is brief. Lio quickly insists that other prisoners remain in Briarhold, redirecting the party toward securing every captive still present.
- The party rescues the remaining prisoners and secures the surviving evidence before Lio and Mara have their fuller conversation.
- The longer exchange happens once everyone is safe, leaving room for relief, anger at Bracken, and the siblings' recognition of what each endured without weakening the urgency of the rescue sequence.

### Fourth Rescued Prisoner

- The fourth prisoner rescued from Briarhold is a Rainroot road warden who noticed organized traffic using routes that were officially closed.
- The cell captured the warden before that observation could be reported through normal channels.
- The warden cannot explain the larger conspiracy, but can identify the western route used to transfer the fifth ledger prisoner beyond Briarhold.
- This testimony provides a grounded lead for the story after Chapter 5 without making the prisoner implausibly knowledgeable.

### Bramblecross Homecoming

- The return to Bramblecross unfolds in two distinct stages.
- First, the rescued party receives a modest public homecoming. The missing cellar porter is restored to the town, while Lio reunites with Enna and Hollis in a scene centered on relief rather than exposition.
- After that emotional closure, the principal characters withdraw to a private evidence meeting with Mayor Anwen and Quill.
- The private meeting assembles the Briarhold records, the Westroot clerk's authentication, the road warden's testimony, and the party's direct account into the chapter's larger conclusion.
- This structure lets Chapter 5 close its personal rescue promises before turning toward the wider conspiracy and the next destination.

### Fifth Prisoner And Political Lead

- The fifth ledger prisoner, already transferred west before the party captures Briarhold, is a route examiner attached to Princess Elowen's Royal Progress.
- The examiner traveled ahead of the Progress to compare official maps, reports, and route orders against conditions witnessed in local communities.
- They discovered that observations from Elowen's Progress were being altered before reaching or leaving the royal road offices. A higher-ranking Briar Crown operation therefore had them removed from Briarhold and sent farther west.
- Lio is the party's primary source for the examiner's identity. The two were held together briefly before the examiner's transfer, allowing Lio to learn their name, role, and the essential fact that they had discovered altered reports.
- Lio does not know the full political structure or the identity of the insider. The surviving transfer ledger, genuine office materials, and Westroot clerk's procedural knowledge corroborate his account.
- At the Bramblecross evidence meeting, these facts show that the conspiracy is intercepting Elowen's inquiry, isolating her from accurate information, and issuing commands in her name.
- The next arc therefore points toward reaching Elowen and uncovering the political machinery around her, with rescuing the known examiner as an immediate human stake. It is not framed simply as pursuing Bracken or chasing an anonymous transfer row.

### Chapter 5 Opening Cadence

- Chapter 5 begins the same night Chapter 4 ends. The prisoners are marked for transfer before dawn, so the story does not insert a full rest, camp, or unexplained time jump.
- A short opening exchange at the captured relay carries the party directly into the rescue attempt.
- The Briarhold Outer Approach map includes the final nighttime ridge crossing rather than adding a separate transitional travel map.
- An abandoned roadworkers' shelter near the approach gives the player one bounded recovery and inventory-preparation opportunity. It does not imply that the party sleeps or abandons the pursuit for hours.
- The opening should preserve urgency while giving players a fair chance to prepare after Chapter 4's relay battle.

### Briarhold Overlook Shelter

- The abandoned roadworkers' shelter sits at a concealed overlook with a useful view of Briarhold, making it a believable place to stop before entering the occupied base.
- The player can rest there once, restoring the player character and active companion to full HP without restoring consumed inventory items or opening a shop.
- The shelter creates an automatic checkpoint and permits unrestricted inventory and equipment management.
- The fiction is a short period of first aid, food, water, and surveillance rather than a full night's sleep.
- Surveying Briarhold from the overlook establishes the receiving gate, guard activity, drainage or runoff evidence leading toward the service culvert, and the station's signal openings.
- The surveillance beat communicates the known risk of each entry approach before the player commits, while leaving room for closer discoveries on the Outer Approach map.

### Physical Outer-Approach Route Choice

- The overlook does not resolve entry through a dialogue-only route-selection menu.
- After surveillance, the player physically descends onto the Outer Approach map and moves toward the chosen entrance.
- Runoff and discarded workshop evidence lead toward the longer service-culvert route.
- The receiving-gate area supports either waiting for supply movement and attempting to slip through with it, or openly confronting the guards.
- The player commits by reaching and interacting with the actual approach. The interface restates the expected alert consequence before the final action.

### Briarhold Party

- The Briarhold rescue party consists of the player character, the player's established chosen companion, and Mara.
- Bramwell, Noma, and Tasmine remain at Westroot's Lower Gate. Their Chapter 4 farewell and gate responsibilities provide the story justification for the smaller team.
- The compact party keeps the stealth sequences readable and preserves the selected companion's importance.
- Mara remains an active story and puzzle participant but not a combatant, consistent with her established role and the Crown Table battle plan.

### Companion-Specific Briarhold Advantages And Voices

- Each established companion provides one meaningful Briarhold advantage without making any route or required discovery exclusive to that companion.
- **Rowan Reedshield** can contain one dangerous encounter, protect Mara, and reduce the escalation caused by a failed confrontation. His dialogue is concise, steady, protective, and tactically observant; he does not boast or turn danger into a joke.
- **Tilda Quickstep** improves concealment and the silent nonlethal ambush against the guard who investigates an invalid lantern signal. Her dialogue is quick, playful, irreverent, and alert to lies or social openings; the humor sharpens rather than trivializes real harm.
- **Moss Fenmere** reads the living-root structure, reveals the service culvert sooner, and can weaken or bypass one thorn mechanism. His dialogue is patient, attentive, and image-rich, but must remain concrete enough that his observations communicate usable information.
- Player stats and choices still determine the route. Companion benefits create different textures and risk profiles rather than an objectively correct selection.
- Chapter 5 writing must provide genuinely companion-specific reactions in each major movement rather than inserting the same interchangeable sentence beneath three names.

### Central Hub And Stealth Presentation

- The party enters Briarhold from a shadowed service landing that gives an immediate view of the False Ledger Room, Thornseal Workshop, Captive Lantern Hall, and heavily guarded inner door.
- The first view clearly communicates the three wings, the rescue objective beyond the inner door, nearby patrol or worker risk, and the current alert state.
- Stealth remains compatible with tile-based movement. It uses authored sightline and noise nodes, visible guard positions, and explicit choice or check dialogues rather than real-time moving-guard simulation.

### Alert Persistence

- Alert ratchets from **Quiet** to **Suspicious** to **Alerted** and cannot be lowered by leaving a room, waiting, or repeating an interaction.
- Wing discoveries can still reduce later resistance even when the facility already knows the party is present.
- Rowan's companion benefit may soften one escalation, but it does not erase established awareness or reset Briarhold to Quiet.

### Wing Occupants And Resolutions

- In the False Ledger Room, the captive Westroot route-record clerk works under a Briar records officer's supervision. The party may distract or quietly incapacitate the officer, or fight openly. Freeing the clerk provides the immediate procedural reading and later authentication of genuine office material.
- In the Thornseal Workshop, a knowingly complicit sealwright works alongside a deceived ordinary laborer. The laborer can be warned or persuaded away; the sealwright can be bypassed, subdued, or fought.
- The workshop contains the shim Mara needs for the locked desk drawer and a separate intervention that prevents a later reinforcement or Thornroot Sentry repair.
- The Captive Lantern Hall is normally unoccupied until a wrong signal draws an investigating guard or the alert state changes its response.

### Captive Release Placement

- The missing Bramblecross cellar porter and Rainroot road warden remain in cells adjoining the Crown Table.
- After Bracken's defeat and Lio's carriage rescue, the party releases them through a straightforward control rather than facing another post-climax puzzle.
- The Westroot clerk may be freed earlier in the Ledger Room. A direct-route player who bypassed that wing must return for the clerk before the chapter permits departure from Briarhold.

### Captive Lantern Puzzle Scale

- The interaction uses three fixed lanterns, each accepting one of four panes distinguished by both color and shape or edge treatment.
- The desk reference shows four meaningful standard signals, including **Front Gate Alert** and the dangerous **Command Center Recall**. Most possible arrangements are meaningless.
- The player may revisit the fixed reference as often as Briarhold's state permits but cannot collect it as an inventory object.

### Chapter 5 Skill Checks

- Slipping through the receiving gate offers two stated approaches: **Precision** to remain unseen or **Heart** to behave like authorized workers.
- Hiding from a guard investigating an invalid signal uses **Instinct**.
- A silent nonlethal ambush uses **Precision**, with Tilda's established companion advantage.
- Each check shows the roll, stat bonus, difficulty, and outcome. Animated player-rolled dice remain deferred to the post-Chapter 5 review.

### Exact Boss Support Baseline

- At **Quiet**, Bracken fights with the Thornroot Sentry.
- At **Suspicious**, one Thornseal Guard joins them.
- At **Alerted**, two Thornseal Guards join them.
- A successful earlier silent ambush removes one applicable guard from the later roster without ever removing Bracken's constant Sentry support.
- The workshop intervention prevents a reinforcement or Sentry repair. Restoring the lantern system weakens Bracken's **False Order** ability.
- HP, damage, and exact turn timing remain graybox tuning values rather than owner-level story decisions.

### Carriage Rescue Interaction

- Bracken's defeat transitions into a short, untimed, three-step interactive rescue rather than another combat encounter.
- Restored gold light reveals the true brake immediately. Without it, false controls create an additional check or injury risk, but no failed input can kill Lio or reverse the authored rescue decision.
- Mara supplies the critical warning, the selected companion assists in character, and Lio continues actively resisting the mechanism.

### Bracken's Escape And The Surviving Evidence

- Bracken escapes wounded with a master routing cipher, preventing the party from immediately exposing every Briar Crown cell or insider.
- The party retains the transfer ledger, a genuine office instruction carrying criminal content, false seal evidence, and the route examiner's transfer record.
- This is enough to break the local cell, prove institutional access, and produce a concrete political lead without prematurely solving the entire conspiracy.

### Lanthorne And Elowen Political Direction

- Princess Elowen remains free and continues her Royal Progress, but her itinerary, reports, and public authority are being manipulated around her.
- The captured route examiner was transferred toward **Lanthorne**, Rainroot's regional seat, where Elowen is due to hold a public hearing.
- The next arc points toward reaching Elowen, finding the examiner, and exposing an attempt to use manufactured road failures against her.
- Chapter 5 reveals the compromised system and immediate political danger without identifying the senior insider.

### Emotional Closing

- Lio and Mara have their fuller private exchange after every captive physically present at Briarhold is safe.
- Lio is angry that Bracken escaped and feels responsible for the examiner's transfer. Mara refuses to let that anger send him out alone.
- The selected companion receives a genuinely voice-specific response to this moment.
- At the Bramblecross case wall, the closing exchange remains: Lio says, **“He is still out there.”** Mara answers, **“So are we.”** Enna's evidence deduction supplies the final political sting.

### Chapter 5 Checkpoints

- Create a checkpoint at the Briarhold overlook shelter.
- Update the infiltration checkpoint after each completed wing while preserving the chosen entry, alert state, expended resources, invalid-signal history, and resolved personnel.
- Create a post-rescue checkpoint before the overland return.

### Progression And Rewards

- Completing the Bracken confrontation and rescue advances the player to level 5.
- The party gains the **True Seal Fragment** and **Briar Chain Link** as story evidence, plus modest supplies and currency from Briarhold.
- Chapter 5 does not award a major weapon that competes with Lio's rescue as the climax's emotional reward.

### Chapter 5 Graybox And Art Process

- Build and playtest the complete Chapter 5 graybox before generating finished map art.
- The graybox must cover all three alert outcomes, every wing dependency, the boss variations, the carriage rescue, and both overland return routes.
- Lock navigable paths, interaction nodes, and player-token alignment before art generation.
- Generate final maps from the accepted scale masters, then visually test each map as soon as it is integrated rather than postponing all alignment review until the full chapter is painted.

## Post-Chapter 5 Backlog

### Ancestry-Aware NPC Reactions

After Chapter 5 is complete, plan a full-story dialogue-reactivity pass based on the player character's ancestry. Shared ancestry can create an easier initial affinity with some NPCs, while comparatively rare peoples such as Cloudlings and Moonmarks may encounter curiosity or suspicion in places where they are unfamiliar.

The later design should preserve individual NPC temperament, regional experience, story choices, and earned trust rather than applying one universal reaction to every member of an ancestry. It should also distinguish unfamiliarity and prejudice from objective truth about any people. Implementation details and the exact mechanical weight remain deliberately deferred until Chapter 5 is finished.

### Player-Rolled Non-Combat Check Presentation

After Chapter 5, prototype an optional presentation in which consequential non-combat skill checks place a die on screen, let the player initiate the roll, animate it, and then reveal the die result, stat bonus, difficulty, and outcome.

The review must weigh tactile agency and readable stakes against the risk that a literal die interrupts story immersion or makes routine interactions feel like a board-game interface. Possible mitigations include reserving the treatment for consequential checks, keeping a quick-resolve option, and styling the roll to match the storybook presentation. Chapter 5 may use dice-driven checks without waiting for this interface.

### Full-Story Companion Differentiation Pass

After Chapter 5, audit the existing story for places where Rowan, Tilda, and Moss can provide additional mechanical, investigative, navigational, or social differences that express their personalities without blocking the critical path or making one companion universally superior.

The same pass should audit companion dialogue voice throughout Chapters 1–5. Rowan should remain concise, protective, and tactically grounded; Tilda quick, playful, and lie-aware; Moss patient, listening-oriented, and image-rich while still communicating clearly. Replace generic interchangeable reactions where a specific perspective could give the chosen companion more flavor.
