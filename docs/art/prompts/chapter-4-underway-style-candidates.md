# Chapter 4 Underway Style Candidates

These prompts establish the visual language for the Chapter 4 Underway before producing the full multi-map set. All three candidates use the Old Keeper Road's accepted navigation geometry so the owner can judge style without also judging a different layout.

- `assets/reference/concept/environments/chapter-4-underway-style-candidates/underway-style-candidate-c-quiet-gouache-v01.png`

The original candidates remain review concepts. Owner-approved Candidate A2 now also has a 1600×900 optimized runtime derivative at `assets/maps/old-keeper-road-map-v01.webp`; it is installed only on the Old Keeper Road for the first lantern-mask lighting proof.

## Shared gameplay constraints

- 16:9 gameplay-map background, not cinematic concept art.
- One continuous road from left to right with no forks or alternate passages.
- Route rhythm follows the accepted `underwayRoute811` overlay: mid-left, high first third, low center-right, high final quarter, mid-right exit.
- Broad, clean road surface for the existing transparent movement overlays.
- No baked-in UI, nodes, grid, labels, icons, characters, enemies, or treasure.
- Base art remains fully painted because the game supplies the moving black lantern mask at runtime.

## Candidate A — Rootwork continuity

Use this direction when continuity with the Root Cellar and Westroot's established root-and-stone visual language matters most.

```text
Create a polished 16:9 gameplay map background for a storybook fantasy adventure game, Candidate A: ROOTWORK CONTINUITY. Orthographic top-down / gently isometric board-game map view, 1600x900 composition. Depict one continuous ancient underground Keeper Road crossing from the far left edge to the far right edge in a pronounced winding S-shaped route. The exact travel rhythm is: left entrance around 56% height, slight drift, climb to 34% and 28% height by the first third, descend through 49% to 74–78% height at center-right, rise again to 57% then 30%, and finish around 52% height at the right edge. The road must remain unmistakably continuous with no forks and no alternate passages. Wide fitted-stone walking surface and broad overlay-safe clearings along the centerline; an old shallow waykeeper rest niche is embedded beside the low central bend, but keep it empty and visually subordinate.

Visual style: warm hand-painted storybook fantasy matching a lovingly illustrated tabletop adventure map; gentle ink-like edge definition, watercolor and gouache texture on paper, subtle brushwork, tactile fitted stone, thick old tree roots quietly embracing masonry, restrained moss and mineral stains. Palette of charcoal blue-gray, umber, muted forest green, weathered brass, and very restrained amber accents. The route should read clearly when only a small moving lantern halo reveals it; use strong local silhouettes and simple value grouping, not tiny details. The base scene itself is dim but still fully painted—do not paint a black fog mask or a fixed circular lantern spotlight into the image, because the game adds a moving darkness mask at runtime. Dark natural rock and root-filled negative space around the road.

Composition constraints: no characters, creatures, enemies, treasure, chests, UI, buttons, tokens, node circles, grid, arrows, compass, text, letters, numbers, signs, labels, border, frame, watermark, or baked-in iconography. No junctions, secret side corridors, doorways implying playable branches, or visible destination room. Avoid photorealism, 3D-render gloss, anime, grimdark horror, neon colors, harsh black outlines, cinematic perspective, and excessive clutter. Deliver a finished environment map background suitable behind transparent navigation overlays.
```

Review note: strongest continuity with existing selected maps. The stone-and-root surroundings are dense, and the generated amber pools should be reduced or removed in a production pass so the moving lantern remains the only authoritative light source.

### Candidate A2 — Unlit rootwork lighting proof

This edit preserves Candidate A's composition while converting it into the cool, matte source plate needed by the game's moving lantern effect.

```text
Edit this exact gameplay-map concept into Candidate A2, an UNLIT ROOTWORK COLOR PLATE for a runtime moving lantern effect. Preserve the existing 16:9 top-down composition, the exact single continuous winding fitted-stone road from left edge to right edge, its width, its S-shaped geometry, the central shallow rest niche, the surrounding roots and rock masses, and the handmade storybook watercolor/gouache character.

Remove every baked-in light source and every visual consequence of a stationary lamp. Specifically remove all golden pools, bright circular road lights, glowing fixtures, illuminated stone patches, directional highlights, cast shadows caused by lamps, and warm light gradients. Any round roadside fittings may remain only as dark, dormant, weathered brass objects with no glow. The empty niche must be unlit. Nothing in the scene emits light.

Repaint the whole environment as a cool, evenly exposed, matte diffuse-color plate: readable blue-charcoal stone, dark umber roots, muted moss, subtle weathered brass, restrained hand-painted value separation, ambient occlusion only in creases and beneath raised edges. Keep the road clearly distinguishable from surrounding rock through local material color and silhouette, not through illumination. Preserve enough midtone information that a runtime circular lantern overlay can warm and brighten whichever small area surrounds the party, while everything outside that runtime halo can be covered by black fog. No vignette and no global darkness painted over the image. No added openings, branches, rooms, characters, creatures, treasure, UI, nodes, grid, symbols, text, labels, signs, border, or watermark. The result should look slightly cool and neutral when viewed alone, intentionally awaiting the game's moving warm lantern light.
```

Review note: owner-approved source-art approach. The standalone image is intentionally brighter and more neutral than the final in-game result; the runtime fog and amber halo are responsible for all perceived lantern illumination.

## Candidate B — Old civic Underway

Use this direction when the Underway should read as forgotten infrastructure built and maintained by an earlier Westroot road service.

```text
Create a polished 16:9 gameplay map background for a storybook fantasy adventure game, Candidate B: OLD CIVIC UNDERWAY. Orthographic top-down / gently isometric board-game map view, 1600x900 composition. Depict one continuous ancient underground Keeper Road crossing from the far left edge to the far right edge in a pronounced winding S-shaped route. The exact travel rhythm is: left entrance around 56% height, slight drift, climb to 34% and 28% height by the first third, descend through 49% to 74–78% height at center-right, rise again to 57% then 30%, and finish around 52% height at the right edge. The road must remain unmistakably continuous with no forks and no alternate passages. Give the fitted road generous width and clean overlay-safe stretches. At the low central bend, include a shallow abandoned waykeeper alcove built into the outer wall—an empty lamp cup and worn brass fixture, no treasure and no readable inscription.

Visual style: hand-painted storybook fantasy with the feeling of forgotten public infrastructure beneath a root-built town. Cooler and older than the cozy Root Cellar: worn blue-slate fitted stones, faded civic geometry, shallow drainage channels, occasional old bronze road studs, hairline cracks, mineral bloom, pale lichen, and restrained roots breaking through later repairs. Gentle ink-like definition, layered watercolor and gouache paper texture, soft matte brushwork, readable board-game-map silhouettes. The masonry should feel designed, maintained long ago, and now quietly decaying—not a natural cave and not a grand ruin. Palette of smoky slate blue, blue-green patina, charcoal, muted ochre, desaturated moss, with sparse dull brass accents. The road must remain legible through a small moving lantern halo, using distinct edge shapes and simple value grouping rather than fine clutter. Base art is dim but fully painted; do not include a black fog mask or a fixed circular spotlight, because runtime adds moving lantern darkness. Dark rock voids and retaining walls should provide calm negative space around the route.

Composition constraints: no characters, creatures, enemies, treasure, chests, UI, buttons, tokens, node circles, grid, arrows, compass, text, letters, numbers, signs, labels, border, frame, watermark, or baked-in iconography. No junctions, alternate corridors, side rooms, stairways implying a playable branch, or visible destination chamber. Avoid photorealism, glossy 3D game art, anime, grimdark horror, steampunk machinery, neon, harsh black linework, cinematic eye-level perspective, excessive debris, and clutter. Deliver a finished environment map background suitable behind transparent navigation overlays.
```

Review note: clearest expression of an engineered Old Keeper Road and the strongest current recommendation. It separates Chapter 4 from the natural Root Cellar without leaving the established storybook map language.

## Candidate C — Quiet gouache storybook

Use this direction when a softer, simpler illustrated-book texture matters more than architectural specificity.

```text
Create a polished 16:9 gameplay map background for a storybook fantasy adventure game, Candidate C: QUIET GOUACHE STORYBOOK. Orthographic top-down board-game map view with only a hint of isometric depth, 1600x900 composition. Depict one continuous ancient underground Keeper Road crossing from the far left edge to the far right edge in a pronounced winding S-shaped route. The exact travel rhythm is: left entrance around 56% height, slight drift, climb to 34% and 28% height by the first third, descend through 49% to 74–78% height at center-right, rise again to 57% then 30%, and finish around 52% height at the right edge. The road must remain unmistakably continuous with no forks or alternate passages. Make the fitted-stone road broad, uncluttered, and overlay-safe. Suggest one small abandoned keeper alcove beside the low central bend using only a simple arched silhouette and empty lamp shelf; it must not compete with the route.

Visual style: a warm, restrained children's fantasy atlas painted in opaque gouache and watercolor on visibly toothy paper. Simpler graphic shapes and broader brush masses than detailed game concept art; softly irregular hand-painted stone blocks; selective fine ink accents only at important edges; gentle pigment blooms, dry-brush grain, and layered matte washes. Preserve a cozy handmade spirit while making the Underway feel hushed, old, and mysterious rather than scary. Palette of deep desaturated indigo, smoky plum-gray, soft umber, muted moss, dusty blue, and tiny touches of old gold. Use calm dark negative space around the winding road and strong shape language so every revealed lantern-sized section is immediately readable. Base art should be low-light but fully painted and evenly exposed; do not add a black fog mask, circular vignette, or fixed lantern spotlight because runtime adds a moving darkness mask. Keep texture broad enough to survive WebP reduction.

Composition constraints: no characters, creatures, enemies, treasure, chests, UI, buttons, tokens, node circles, grid, arrows, compass, text, letters, numbers, signage, labels, border, frame, watermark, or baked-in iconography. No forks, side corridors, doors suggesting playable branches, stairs, or destination chambers. Avoid photorealism, high-detail digital matte painting, glossy 3D rendering, anime, grimdark horror, neon, hard comic outlines, cinematic eye-level perspective, ornate fantasy architecture, and visual clutter. Deliver a finished environment map background suitable behind transparent navigation overlays.
```

Review note: appealing painterly color and the softest illustrated-book atmosphere, but the generated image drifts toward a grand cave vista. It also introduces fixed lanterns and apparent background openings. Treat it as a texture-and-palette reference rather than a production-ready composition.

## Current recommendation

Proceed from Candidate A2's rootwork and fitted-stone language. Keep all future Underway map sources cool, matte, and free of fixed light sources; let the runtime party lantern supply every warm pool and visible falloff. Candidate B remains a useful secondary reference for old civic details. Do not carry Candidate C's fixed lanterns or implied cave branches into production.

## Construction detour paired-route proof

The first production follow-up tests the approved rootwork language against the much straighter, visibly newer construction detour. Its concept source is preserved at `assets/reference/concept/environments/chapter-4-underway-style-candidates/construction-detour-map-candidate-v01.png`; the runtime derivative is `assets/maps/construction-detour-map-v01.webp`.

```text
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: 16:9 gameplay map background for a tile/node-based storybook fantasy adventure
Primary request: Create the Chapter 4 construction detour as the visual counterpart to the winding Old Keeper Road. It must be one continuous, nearly straight underground passage crossing from the far left edge to the far right edge, with no branches. The accepted movement centerline stays around the vertical middle: begin near 54% height at x=5%, then 53%, 52%, 51%, 50%, 49% at center, and gently return through 50%, 51%, 52%, ending near 53% height at x=95%. The deviation should be subtle—clearly much straighter and newer than a winding old road.
Scene/backdrop: a recently cut construction passage beneath Westroot, driven through dark rock and older roots. Broad chiseled walking floor, fresh retaining courses, practical squared timber-and-iron braces, shallow drainage grooves, numbered-looking but completely blank tag plates, small piles of controlled rubble outside the walk line, and roots cleanly trimmed back from the new cut. At roughly 55% across and 49% height, include a readable but compact live construction signal rig: a wall-mounted pulley, taut cord, counterweight, and mechanical indicator arm connected westward. It must be an unlit mechanical discovery, not a lamp, machine room, or steampunk device.
Style/medium: the same handmade Liam's Game storybook map language as the approved unlit rootwork direction—orthographic top-down with only gentle isometric depth, watercolor and opaque gouache texture on toothy paper, soft matte brushwork, lightly irregular ink-like edge definition, tactile stone, roots, wood, and weathered metal. Family-friendly fantasy, practical rather than spectacular.
Composition/framing: 1672x941-style 16:9 landscape, full environment background behind transparent movement overlays. Keep the central passage broad and clean. The route must remain unmistakably continuous and almost horizontal. Dark rock and root masses form calm negative space above and below. No apparent side corridors, doors, caves, stairs, or alternate routes.
Lighting/mood: UNLIT DIFFUSE COLOR PLATE. Nothing emits light. No lanterns, torches, flames, glowing crystals, lit fixtures, golden pools, spotlight, vignette, cast shadows from a point source, or warm illumination gradients. Paint cool, evenly exposed material colors and restrained ambient occlusion only, intentionally awaiting the game's moving party-lantern overlay at runtime.
Color palette: cool blue-charcoal fitted stone and raw rock, dark umber roots, muted moss, desaturated timber brown, blackened iron, sparse dull brass. Slightly newer, cleaner stone than the Old Keeper Road, but still handmade and storybook.
Constraints: no characters, creatures, enemies, treasure, chests, UI, buttons, tokens, node circles, grid, arrows, compass, readable text, letters, numbers, signs, labels, border, frame, watermark, or baked-in iconography. No junctions or playable branches. No fixed light sources. Keep important floor stretches overlay-safe and preserve strong local silhouettes within a small moving lantern halo.
Avoid: photorealism, glossy 3D game art, anime, grimdark horror, steampunk spectacle, modern asphalt or road paint, mine-cart rails, neon, cinematic eye-level perspective, ornate ruins, excessive debris, and visual clutter.
```

Review note: the first generation established the intended route contrast, signal rig, and unlit treatment, but owner playtesting found its floor too broad to imply a long passage. V02 narrows the complete constructed corridor to roughly 13–15% of the canvas height. The original runtime WebP is preserved at `assets/reference/alternates/assets/maps/construction-detour-map-v01.webp`; V02 is the selected runtime map.

## Underway approach production map

The first Chapter 4 traversal map is deliberately pre-choice: it shows the Lower Gate approach and the legitimate-looking closure, but neither the Old Keeper Road nor the construction detour. The folded-map result therefore reveals useful route information instead of duplicating branches already visible in the environment.

```text
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: 16:9 gameplay environment map for a tile/node-based storybook fantasy adventure
Primary request: Create the Chapter 4 Lower Gate approach inside an ancient subterranean passage. A single continuous fitted-stone path runs from the far left to the far right, gently winding upward through the middle and then back down. At the far left is an old iron Lower Gate leaf embedded in roots and stone. Near the left-middle is a subtle bronze route-dial mechanism built into the wall or floor. At the far right, the passage ends visually at a legitimate-looking construction closure board or barrier made of aged wood and dull bronze, with no readable writing. It should feel like an official closure that prompts the player to inspect a map.
Gameplay geometry: one path only. Do not show any fork, branch, side passage, maintenance route, detour corridor, alternate road, or hidden opening. Keep traversable space aligned to this approximate screen-space centerline: (6%,57%), (18%,55%), (30%,51%), (43%,39%), (57%,34%), (70%,46%), (82%,53%), (94%,56%). Leave clear path space for overlaid player tokens and UI.
Lighting: unlit source art. Cool, matte, diffuse colors with deep blue-gray stone, muted brown roots, restrained dull bronze, and soft painted value separation. No baked-in lantern glow, warm pool of light, torches, lamps, magical illumination, bright highlights, or dramatic vignette. All apparent light is supplied at runtime by the party's movable lantern.
Style and camera: top-down with a gentle storybook/isometric tilt; tactile roots woven through ancient masonry; painterly watercolor and gouache texture; richly painted but highly readable gameplay terrain; cohesive with a premium illustrated children's fantasy book.
Constraints: no characters, creatures, tokens, footprints, labels, text, arrows, UI, grid lines, readable signs, glowing objects, or visible fork. The closure barrier may have abstract weathered marks but no letters or symbols.
```

Review note: the initial generation established the correct pre-choice composition, but its large paving and architectural units made the route feel like a short platform. V02 scaled down the floorstones and landmarks, but still relied on a shallow route and retained an unexplained bronze route dial. It is preserved in reference alternates with V01.

## Approved-scale Underway map suite

The approach V03 became the binding scale master for the full Chapter 4 tunnel journey on 2026-08-20. Later maps preserve these rules:

- one continuous left-to-right road per background; merges and unused branches remain offscreen
- road width approximately 8–10% of canvas height, with many small fitted stones and small physical landmarks
- length expressed by actual cumulative bends as well as small-scale detail
- a hierarchy of massive structural roots, medium braided retaining roots, and fine tendrils
- cool matte unlit source color; runtime supplies every warm lantern effect
- no baked characters, enemies, tokens, labels, readable signs, or route-choice UI
