# Chapter 2 Crown Door Dungeon and Three-Door Visual Prompts

Use these prompts for ChatGPT image generation. They follow the current Liam's Game storybook art direction and avoid baked-in UI text so the game can add labels separately.

# Crown Door Dungeon Map Prompt

```text
Create a hand-painted storybook fantasy gameplay dungeon map for "Liam's Game," Chapter 2: the Crown Door Roadwatcher Den. The image should be a top-down / slightly isometric painted map background designed for tile-based movement, not a poster or cinematic scene.

Scene: a hidden dungeon behind the false Crown Door at the Three-Door Threshold. This is not a royal castle. It is a practical den where Briar Crown roadwatchers manufacture false road commands, copied seals, altered sign slats, and thorn-collar supplies. It should feel organized, secretive, and dangerous, but still family-friendly and storybook adventurous rather than horror.

Include these readable gameplay rooms arranged as distinct connected spaces:
- Crown Vestibule: an entry chamber with a too-official false crown door, six exaggerated handles, root-stone walls, and blank order boards.
- Wax Table: a workroom with pine-pitch wax, green Willow wax scraps, crown stamps, crate tags, seal cloth, and small tools.
- Slat Rack: racks of half-painted road signs and removable wooden slats, some with old lantern marks partly scraped away.
- Witness Ledger Nook: a tucked-away desk or root alcove with route ledgers, witness notes, string, and small courier marks.
- Collar Kennel: a small side room with broken thorn collars and soft bedding for a freed hound; threatening but not cruel or gory.
- False Map Room: a central or final room with a distorted road map made of strings, pins, sign slats, and root lines, showing how the straight road has been edited.
- Exit / return route: a subtle passage or root stair leading back toward the Three-Door Threshold.

Style: warm children's fantasy storybook painting, watercolor/gouache texture, soft brushwork, gentle ink-like definition, cozy but mysterious lantern light, readable silhouettes, tactile wood/stone/root materials, no modern objects. Use earthy greens, root browns, warm gold lantern light, muted parchment, pine-wax amber, and small controlled accents of false crown red. The false authority should feel invasive and handmade, not grand or ornate.

Composition requirements:
- 16:9 landscape, suitable for a React game map background.
- Leave slight safe margins around all edges.
- Make paths, doorways, and rooms align visually to a tile/node-based dungeon layout.
- Keep room centers visually clean enough for game tokens and interactable labels.
- Avoid readable text, UI panels, characters as the focus, gore, horror, heavy darkness, logos, watermarks, photorealism, 3D render style, anime, or clutter that obscures the walkable paths.

Output as a high-resolution PNG with crisp details and layered-looking brushwork. The player should be able to understand where to walk, where to stop, and which rooms are important.
```

Negative prompt:

```text
No photorealism, no 3D render, no anime, no horror, no grimdark palette, no readable text labels, no logo, no watermark, no UI frame, no modern tools, no gore, no torture imagery, no character closeups, no heavy fog hiding landmarks, no clutter that obscures paths.
```

# Three-Door Threshold Visual Prompt

```text
Create a storybook fantasy illustration for "Liam's Game," Chapter 2: the Three-Door Threshold. This should be a wide dialogue/scene visual showing three distinct doors set side by side into an old forest hillside beneath tangled roots. The image should clearly show all three doors at once, with enough detail that each can later be cropped or used for closer door views.

The three doors:
- Left: the Crown Door. Tall, straight, too official, newly painted, with a crooked false crown motif and too many handles or oversized handles that practically demand to be pulled. It should feel bossy and suspicious, not truly royal.
- Center: the Lantern Door. Lower, older, weathered, kind, and quiet, with a small lantern mark, moss, worn stone, and a sense of guidance rather than command.
- Right: the No-Handle Door. Smooth stone grown into roots, blank at first glance, no handle, no keyhole, no obvious seam. Near the base, include a tiny hook-tailed courier mark that an observant viewer might notice, but do not make it the main focus.

Mood: mysterious, inviting, and tense in a family-friendly way. The scene should feel like an old road asking a question. The Crown Door should be visually loud, the Lantern Door quietly helpful, and the No-Handle Door honest but hidden.

Style: warm children's fantasy storybook painting, watercolor/gouache texture, soft brushwork, gentle ink-like definition, handmade illustrated-book feeling, cozy gold-green underground light, earthy moss and root textures, clear silhouettes, no slick digital look.

Composition requirements:
- 16:9 landscape.
- Camera at slight front-facing angle, not fully top-down.
- All three doors visible in one frame with balanced spacing.
- Leave some clean space near the lower edge for game dialogue/UI overlays.
- No characters in the foreground. If any scale hints are included, keep them subtle, such as small stones, leaves, or travel marks.
- Do not include readable text labels, captions, UI, logo, watermark, photorealism, 3D render style, anime, horror, extreme darkness, or heavy fog.

Output as a high-resolution PNG. Make the doors distinct enough that a player immediately wants to approach each one for a closer look.
```

Negative prompt:

```text
No readable words or labels, no UI, no logo, no watermark, no photorealism, no 3D render, no anime, no horror, no grimdark lighting, no modern hinges or hardware, no castle throne-room look, no characters blocking the doors, no blurry atmospheric-only scene.
```
