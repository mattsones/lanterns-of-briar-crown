# Liam's Game — Story Writing Rules

These rules apply to runtime dialogue, canonical chapter scripts, story bibles, quest copy, playtest fixtures, and art prompts.

## Character Pronouns

1. Every singular person uses **he/him** or **she/her** pronouns.
2. Do not use singular **they/them/their** for a character, an unnamed individual, a generic traveler, or a role such as *the runner*.
3. Use **they/them/their** only for a clearly plural antecedent: two or more people, a group, or multiple objects.
4. If a new character's gender has not been decided, decide and record it before writing prose. Do not use singular *they* as a placeholder.
5. Prefer a person's name or role when repeating *he* or *she* would make a crowded scene unclear.

## Clarity Before Metaphor

Write scenes in a descriptive, immersive style. Establish the physical surroundings, then let sustained action and conversation carry the party through them. Vary sentence length; reserve short, clipped sentences for moments that earn that rhythm. Give quiet reunions and long journeys room to unfold through gestures, sensory detail, and changing surroundings, with concrete information earned through the characters' experience.

State the physical action, speaker, location, and consequence before adding figurative language. When a choice changes an outcome, name the causal link in the choice label and the resulting prose.

Spell out a number at the beginning of a sentence. Numeric values may remain in labels, tables, and compact combat/stat displays.

## Narrative Perspective

Keep story prose inside the player's current experience. Describe what the party observes and does, using the knowledge earned so far.

- When revising a scene, remove the mistaken detail rather than adding a denial of it. The current story should stand on its own, without explanations aimed at an earlier draft.
- Let the physical scene establish cover, locked objects, restraints, and access. Avoid repeating those facts as narratorial reminders of what the player cannot do or could not see earlier.
- Keep a restriction or warning when it helps the player understand a present choice, hazard, or consequence. Explain relevant checks and changed effects clearly; omit unsolicited reassurance about hypothetical failures, repeat encounters, or rejected story alternatives.
- Introduce an enemy through observable behavior. Keep named attacks, exact penalties, and repair rules out of the encounter's introductory story prose; combat feedback can explain an effect once the player sees it happen.
