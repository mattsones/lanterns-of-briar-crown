# Chapter 3 Story Truth

Last updated: 2026-07-27

Status: Approved story foundation implemented in the canonical Chapter 3 script and playable runtime, including the Witherdeath, sanitation, transfer, hold-scope, and Bramblecross-compact decisions. A fresh human comprehension playthrough remains the final release-candidate signoff.

## Westroot And Bramblecross

Westroot is a hidden western junction of the old Lantern Road. From Bramblecross, the road reaches Westroot and then divides toward deeper western routes and branches serving several settlements. Westroot coordinates those connections through witnessed cargo, courier passages, shelter, water, warnings, and route records.

About sixty years ago, the two-year Witherdeath epidemic spread through Alderreach, Veyrun, and neighboring countries. Westroot's Transfer Checkpoint then verified identity and cargo obligations but had no orderly health checks or sanitation procedures. The illness entered through ordinary admitted traffic and killed residents.

The part of Split Hall favoring strict order won the political response. Westroot ended routine surface compacts, disappeared from public maps, stopped ordinary surface traffic, and rebuilt the checkpoint around washing, airing, inspection, separated cargo handling, and short observation. Strong sanitation was prudent; total civic isolation was an overreaction that became the new norm. Most partner settlements lost formal contact. Bramblecross, once Westroot's nearest active surface partner, gradually came to regard it as a story.

Westroot did not become inert. It maintained the outer cistern shelter and admitted rare essential transfers through the First Gate. A normal arrival requires a named outside account, confirmation from a Westroot gatekeeper, and checkpoint sanitation. Travelers see only the threshold and assigned route. The older phrase-operated opening used by Liam's party predates this account system and has not admitted an unscheduled surface party in living memory.

## The Briar Operation

The Briar Crown wants Westroot because it joins roads beneath several settlements. The faction can use it to move false orders, supplies, agents, and prisoners while keeping towns from comparing what they know.

Its recent convoy combines two stolen forms of trust: Ada's Willow seal makes the crate look like legitimate surface cargo, while a copied Westroot account mark and stolen second-watch timing make the movement look authorized inside. A Briar cargo operative acknowledges the transfer and remains hidden near Cargo Siding. Westroot as a community does not knowingly admit the faction.

## Lio And The Crate

Three nights before Liam's party arrives, the false convoy enters through the First Gate. Lio is restrained, hooded, and marched beside it under an unnamed **westward relay transfer**. He is never inside the crate.

The convoy first stops at the Transfer Checkpoint on the broad inner landing of the First Gate. This is where Westroot washes and inspects admitted travelers and cargo, then compares outside papers with its own account before permitting movement beyond the threshold. While the handlers wait, Lio scratches his hook-tailed mark at the gate: **Alive past this point. Do not trust the straight road.**

A Mossback child helping Auntie Lume serves the checkpoint's ordinary rootbread tray. Every person held there receives bread and water, even before Westroot decides whether to admit them. The Briar handlers allow the routine because refusing Westroot hospitality would make their transfer conspicuous. Lio eats, ties blue courier thread around the cup in his hooked knot, and returns it on the tray. The child sets the unfamiliar cup aside at the checkpoint.

The convoy moves Lio through the Lower West Gate toward Briarhold Waystation. The crate remains in Cargo Siding with forged orders, seal tools, restraints, route-scratching tools, Lio's confiscated pouch, and the transfer tag that reduced him to a freight entry.

Bramwell later finds that the Willow seal and sender record disagree. He holds the crate and closes the Witness Stone walk and listening marks during review. The hidden operative then moves the crate behind the rear stacks, brushes its tracks, and resets the lock. Its apparent disappearance triggers the Hold Bell.

## The Chapter's Decisions

The party's arrival alarms Westroot because the First Gate opens without a scheduled account, inside confirmation, or Gatehand key—not because the gate has never opened before.

The Rootbread Promise means that a guarded boundary may delay passage but must not make a hungry person invisible. Auntie Lume tells the party that the child served an unnamed courier during the false Willow transfer. The player returns to the Transfer Checkpoint, hears the child's account, and Mara identifies Lio's knot on the returned cup. Liam and Mara then restock the checkpoint tray for the next traveler. The discovery is that **the Rootbread Promise reached Lio**; the player's action continues the promise rather than retroactively keeping it.

The chapter has two distinct reopening decisions:

1. At the Witness Stones, Bramwell and Noma lift the limited hold on the stone walk and listening marks. Westroot answers the outer shelter and authorizes a witnessed Cargo Siding investigation. This does not reopen Westroot generally to Bramblecross.
2. After hearing the cargo evidence, Split Hall establishes its first restored surface compact with Bramblecross. Isolation allowed the false Willow credentials to go unchecked; Liam's party can now name Ada, Enna, Hollis, and records against which the next claim can be verified. The First Gate remains guarded; people and cargo require names and witnesses; outward warnings and shelter signals resume. Quill carries the first written warning to Enna. Initially only Enna, Hollis, and named watch or courier contacts learn the route. Other former partners are not automatically readmitted.

## Purposeful Mysteries

The player may still wonder how the Briar operative first obtained the mark and timing, whether anyone exposed them unknowingly, what the faction needs from Lio, where he is inside Briarhold, and how large the network is.

Whether Lio was inside the crate, how he returned his string at the checkpoint, why the party's gate opening caused alarm, and what Split Hall decided are facts—not mysteries.

## Map Placement

The Transfer Checkpoint is a sub-location of the **First Westroot Gate**, not a separate village landmark. It occupies the broad inner landing already painted just beyond the no-handle door. Its eventual art or map dressing should show a wash basin or water channel, an airing rack, an inspection bench, an account rail, and the ordinary Rootbread tray without turning sanitation into a new puzzle. The player encounters it during arrival, then returns after Lume supplies the Rootbread lead. A temporary Mossback-child marker at the gate landing makes that later interaction visible. The existing sealed-hatch landmark is retired rather than explained.
